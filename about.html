My journey

From visual craft to product strategy, systems, and leadership.

A journey shaped by changing questions — from how something should look, to how it should work, scale, and create meaningful outcomes.

2015 · Beginning

Starting with visual design

My career started with a strong foundation in visual and graphic design. It taught me something I still carry today: details matter. Typography, hierarchy, composition, spacing, and visual communication became the foundation for how I think about interfaces.

2017 · UX

Moving into UI & UX

As I worked on more digital products, I became increasingly interested in what happens before the pixels. I started exploring user flows, information architecture, interaction design, usability, and prototyping.

The question changed from “How should this look?” to “How should this work?”

2019 · Product

Becoming a product designer

I began taking ownership of problems end-to-end — from understanding requirements and users to defining experiences, designing interfaces, prototyping, and collaborating with engineering and product teams.

This was where design became much more than an execution role for me.

2021 · Enterprise

Designing for complex products

Enterprise products exposed me to a different kind of design challenge: multiple personas, complex workflows, large information spaces, business rules, legacy systems, and different levels of access and responsibility.

Good enterprise UX isn't about removing complexity. It’s about organizing complexity so people can understand and act on it.

2020 · Scale

Design systems & scale

As products and teams grew, I started focusing more deeply on design systems, reusable patterns, consistency, accessibility, and scalable product foundations.

The question became: “How do we design not just one great screen, but a system that enables hundreds of great experiences?”

2022 · Lead

Becoming a design lead

I stepped into design leadership, working at the intersection of product strategy, UX, systems thinking, visual design, and team leadership. I began leading design initiatives, mentoring designers, collaborating closely with product and engineering, and creating clarity across complex product work.

I’m particularly interested in how AI is changing the way products are designed, built, and experienced — and how designers can move from simply designing interfaces to designing intelligent systems and experiences.
