Enterprise UX

PropChk

Simplifying a complex role-based property inspection platform.

A redesign of a data-heavy web application used to manage property inspections, defects, teams, projects, and quality information across multiple roles.

My role

Lead Product Designer

Timeline

4 weeks · 31 days

Platform

Web application

UX audit · Information architecture · User flows · Wireframes · Design system · Visual design

The challenge

The product worked. The experience had become complicated.

The existing application had multiple personas, workflows, and large amounts of operational data.

As the product evolved, navigation became difficult and important information could take several interactions to reach.

Simplify the experience without removing the functionality different roles depended on.

UX audit

Before redesigning, we audited what was already there.

Navigation

Where do users go to complete important tasks?

Information

Is the right information visible at the right time?

Interaction

Are actions and next steps clear?

Roles

How should the experience adapt to different personas?

Data

Can users quickly understand what they're seeing?

What we found

The complexity wasn't just visual. It was structural.

Too many interactions

Important information could take multiple steps to reach.

Dense information

Large amounts of data competed for attention.

Role-based complexity

Different personas needed different information and actions.

Inconsistent hierarchy

Important actions weren't always easy to identify.

Data-heavy screens

Charts, tables, and metrics needed clearer representation.

The redesign therefore focused on navigation, information hierarchy, role-based flows, and data representation.

From findings to UX

We translated the audit into four design principles.

Reduce the path

Make important tasks easier to reach.

Prioritise information

Show users what matters first.

Adapt to the role

Surface relevant information and actions.

Keep it consistent

Create one predictable experience across the product.

Roles

One product. Multiple roles.

The product supported seven personas, each with different responsibilities. Rather than creating separate experiences, we established a shared foundation.

Shared

Navigation · Layout · Components · Interaction patterns

Adapted

Designing for data

The problem wasn't a lack of information. It was clarity.

The product contained inspection, project, revenue, defect, quality, and performance data. We improved how this information was communicated.

Clearer hierarchyChartsGraphsStatus indicators TablesVisual grouping

Metrics, trends and deficiency breakdowns grouped so operational data can be scanned at a glance.

Design foundation

One visual language across multiple roles.

We moved towards reusable components and patterns so that different personas could have role-specific experiences without making the product feel fragmented.

The final visual direction used DM Sans with a defined colour palette and introduced improvements to alignment, CTAs, back navigation, charts, and graphs.

Final experience

A clearer experience for a complex product.

Simpler navigation

Reduce unnecessary interaction.

Better hierarchy

Make important information easier to find.

Clearer data

Make operational information easier to scan.

Role-based flexibility

Adapt the experience to different responsibilities.

Consistency

Create one cohesive product language.

Outcome

A more structured foundation for a multi-role product.

The redesign brought together improved navigation, clearer information hierarchy, better data representation, role-based flows, reusable components, and a consistent visual language.

The project was completed in 31 days, covering audit, flows, wireframes, design system, visual design, and prototyping.

Reflection

Complexity doesn't always need to be removed. It needs to be organised.

The goal wasn't to simplify the product by removing complexity. It was to simplify the experience of navigating that complexity.
